How these auctions work
  • Cash only, paid in full at the winning bid. No cards.
  • No keys. Cars are sold as is, with no warranty and no refunds.
  • A listed lienholder stays on the car after the sale and can repossess it.
  • Remove the car by 5:00pm or storage runs $20 a day. After 96 hours it's treated as abandoned.
  • You get a Certificate of Sale to take to the DMV for title. The DMV may require a salvage exam.

Lists come from the NYC Finance auctions page and refresh at 6am and 6pm.

Model, trim, engine and safety equipment are decoded from each VIN by NHTSA. When the city's year and the VIN disagree, the card shows the VIN year and says so.

Photos are stock exterior shots of the same model from Wikimedia Commons and Wikipedia, not the car on the lot. The exact year is used when one exists. Otherwise the photo is labeled with the year it shows.

Safety stars, recalls and complaints cover the whole model year, not this car. Use the Recalls link to see whether this car's recalls were done. NHTSA often files a model under its own name, shown under Details. No data means NHTSA has nothing under that model and year.

Vehicle history lists past auction listings of the exact VIN found on AmericaMotors, autousa.pro and BidHistory, checked during each update, soonest sale first. They show the car as it was then, not today. No record found is not a clean history.

Mileage, color and accident history are not in a VIN. History searches the VIN, which often turns up old Copart or IAA listings with photos and odometer readings.
